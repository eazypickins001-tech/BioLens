**The single biggest finding:** the niche is split between low-detail stock-footage
recaps and talking-head anatomy. Almost nobody owns a consistent **optical**
identity — macro texture, magnification, focus pulls, clinical but warm colour.
The biology is treated as content; the looking is treated as an afterthought.

**The second finding:** the winning length is not the platform maximum. Posts that
land a single mechanism in under 35 seconds out-retain posts that stack three
facts. BioLens is built for one mechanism per post, delivered in 32 seconds.

## 3. The mechanics everyone in this niche shares

1. One subject, one thesis — never a listicle.
2. Superlative plus contradiction in the first line.
3. Numeric specificity — numbers read as evidence.
4. A hidden world the viewer has never seen, even if they own the body it happens in.
5. Authority through specificity — species names, measurements, named structures.
6. Identity binding — the viewer is the subject wherever possible.
7. An open loop in the first two seconds, closed in the final third.
8. Serialisation: a recurring frame, label or sign-off.
9. A second business behind the channel — courses, presets, a prompt library.

**Ours:**

1. One mechanism per post, never a list of facts.
2. The hook is the most impossible true sentence in the research.
3. Numbers are spoken only when they change the meaning, never as decoration.
4. Every post makes an invisible process visible with a named mechanism.
5. Species names and structures are correct; sources sit in the description.
6. Second person wherever the process happens inside a body; "this animal"
   where the subject is non-human.
7. The open loop closes on screen in clip 3 or 4, never in the caption only.
8. The lens-iris wipe, magnification chip and sign-off are identical every post.
9. A prompt and preset library for creators who want the same look.

## 5. Format spec (do not deviate)

- **Length:** 32 s content default (4 clips × 8 s). Range: 24–35 s social,
  40–48 s story. Published runtime adds intro and outro.
- **Cadence:** 5 posts per week. One story post, four social posts.
- **Clip limit:** Veo 8 s per clip, Omni 10 s per clip. This project uses 8 s
  clips on Veo, and uses Omni only when a single continuous mechanism needs 10 s.
- **Structure:**
  - `GRAB` — the interrupt, the impossible first frame, hook spoken inside 1.5 s
  - `MECHANISM` — the named process, one irreversible action
  - `ESCALATION` — the reveal that makes the first claim stranger
  - `PAYOFF + CTA` — the promise delivered, then one line
- **Visual grammar:** all footage is AI-generated biology; all text, numbers,
  labels, magnification chips and scale rulers are editor-built; no archive is
  ever AI-substituted.
- **Text:** always in the editor, never from the model.
- **Series:** every post belongs to a named series so the catalogue compounds.

## 7. Integrity guardrails (project-specific additions)

1. No claim about human behaviour or cognition from an animal study unless the
   study itself measured humans. Toxoplasma is framed as rodent biology.
2. No medical advice. Human posts describe mechanisms, never diagnosis or cure.
3. No "scientists are close to a cure" language. If a result is early, say early.
4. Every number that carries the hook gets a source in the description.
5. No gore for shock. Visceral is a texture, not a wound.

---

## 8. What this channel will never do

1. Never write a listicle or stack two mechanisms in one post.
2. Never open with "in this video" or a logo before the interrupt.
3. Never use a familiar human fact with a big number as a substitute for an
   alien mechanism.
4. Never let the model render text, numbers or labels.
5. Never fake a study, a screenshot, a quote or a record.
6. Never repeat a topic already in the bank; mine the deep sections instead.
